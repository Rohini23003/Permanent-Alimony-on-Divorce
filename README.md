## Topic
Permanent alimony on divorce represents a cornerstone of matrimonial jurisprudence, dictating the financial security and standard of living of spouses post-dissolution. This topic interests our team due to the evolving judicial standards under Section 25 of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, and the frequent invocation of Article 142 of the Constitution to settle long-standing matrimonial disputes equitably.

## How We Found the Judgments
We utilized the Supreme Court of India judgments portal and SCC Online database, applying search queries combining "permanent alimony", "maintenance", and "irretrievable breakdown". We screened judgments delivered between 1 September 2021 and 31 August 2026, filtering for reasoned substantive decisions and excluding procedural orders or notices, resulting in 21 comprehensive judgments.

## What the Data Shows

![Judgments per year](chart1-per-year.png)
The distribution of judgments across years demonstrates a sharp increase in reported adjudications through 2024 and 2025 (totaling 21 reasoned judgments). It surprised us how heavily the docket concentrated in recent years, reflecting increased appellate scrutiny on quantum uniformity.

![Outcome breakdown](chart2-outcome.png)
The outcome breakdown indicates that a significant majority of petitions are either fully allowed or partly allowed with upward modifications of alimony. Dismissals are rare as parties typically approach the apex court after protracted litigation seeking final closures.

![Type of case](chart3-type.png)
Type of case analysis reveals that Civil Appeals and Special Leave Petitions form the vast majority of alimony and divorce appeals, with criminal appeals and other matters addressing maintenance and domestic violence intersections.

## Limits
A reader should not conclude definitive nationwide trial statistics or universal fixed brackets for alimony from 21 Supreme Court judgments. Because the apex court selectively hears appeals involving specific legal disputes or quantum disagreements, these cases represent outlier matrimonial friction rather than standard family court outcomes across India.
